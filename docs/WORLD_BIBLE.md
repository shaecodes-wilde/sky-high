# World Bible: Cloudbloom - A Sky Out of Tune

Working title for the Sky High project.

Last updated: 2026-10-13 (America/Halifax).
Status: overarching story agreed; individual chapters and levels remain in development.

## 1. How to Use This Document

This is the main reference for ongoing worldbuilding. It records the decisions made in the October 13 discussion and preserves concepts from the supplied character file and conversation transcript.

Status labels:
- **Confirmed:** explicitly selected by the user during the discussion.
- **Source concept:** an idea preserved from the supplied files, awaiting detailed chapter or design approval.
- **Proposed:** a suggested connection or implementation detail that has not been selected.
- **Open:** a decision still to make.
- **Superseded:** an earlier idea replaced by a later explicit choice.

Confirmed decisions take precedence over source concepts and proposals. This document describes intended narrative direction; it does not claim that the story, characters, or worlds have been implemented in the game.

## 2. The Premise

**Confirmed:** Poppy and Sir Puddlewick come from separate universes and accidentally arrive in the same dimension through separate encounters with Zuzu the Zipperfish's little universe.

The destination is naturally woven from different worlds. Its existence is independent of the missing morning song.

Poppy is content at home and curious about what lies beyond it. Puddlewick is a self-appointed knight whose grand title helps him feel important. Their shared curiosity carries them into adventures with unusual new friends.

They first become involved in the missing song by helping Auntie Aster. Separate local troubles have pulled the melody apart, leaving dawn faltering across the dimension. Helping inhabitants and moving through their environments brings those places into Bloom and reveals fragments of the song.

Their journey reaches the sleeping sun, a gentle living presence. Restoring the melody wakes the sun and brings a full dawn. Afterward, Poppy and Puddlewick return to their separate homes, with their friendship intact and future visits possible.

## 3. Confirmed Story Decisions

### Emotional Direction and Audience

- Discovery and adventure are the primary emotional heart.
- Friendship grows through shared exploration and experience.
- The tone is wonder with bittersweet depth: playful, surreal environments can hold meaningful character conflicts and a tender farewell.
- The audience is all ages, with accessible storytelling and emotional depth that also resonates with adults.
- Story delivery uses brief scenes and rich worlds: short conversations and expressive environments carry most of the plot, with a few longer moments at major turns.

### Poppy

- Poppy is content but curious before travelling.
- She stays joyfully herself through the adventure. A major insecurity or corrective personal transformation is not required.
- Her curiosity and kindness remain steady while her experiences and friendships expand.
- Her everyday dynamic with Puddlewick is curiosity and ceremony: she approaches things openly and takes his sincerity seriously.

**Source concept / open detail:** The transcript identifies Poppy as a mushroom character and suggests a woodland village opening. Her precise home, history, appearance, and opening scene still need development.

### Sir Puddlewick

- Puddlewick is a self-appointed knight.
- He invented the title to feel important and learns that his friends value him without a grand reputation.
- His elaborate manners and interpretation of events as a grand quest provide comedy.
- The stained-glass duck church and Holy Duck Trinity remain part of his home culture.
- Duck-oil sales are dropped from his current character direction.

**Open:** His home universe, personal history, the Trinity's identities, and the church chapter's specific events are not yet defined. No official knighting or change of title has been decided.

### Travel and the Woven Dimension

- The protagonists begin in separate universes.
- Their arrivals are accidental.
- Zuzu's little universe is the selected arrival mechanism.
- Different worlds being woven into this dimension is natural and longstanding.
- The missing song is a disruption within that existing dimension; it did not create the weaving.
- The detailed mechanics of the two encounters with Zuzu are open.

### The Morning Song and Local Conflicts

- The song is a scattered melody, rather than a living chorus or a shared morning ritual.
- Independent local troubles pulled its different parts apart.
- Dawn is faltering: morning arrives unevenly, and each region loses some colour, rhythm, or bloom in its own way.
- There is no single central antagonist responsible for every local trouble.
- The quest starts by helping one new friend, specifically Auntie Aster.
- Poppy and Puddlewick want to explore before going home. They are not undertaking a primarily escape-driven quest.
- Helping a character and moving through their world brings it back to life through Bloom, revealing a melody fragment to carry onward.

**Open:** The exact magical process by which local troubles separated the melody, the physical form of fragments, and their relationship to existing in-game collectibles remain to be defined.

### Friends, Chapters, and the Finale

- The central pair travels together.
- Other friends mainly inhabit their own regions, recur during the journey, and reunite for the finale.
- Selected main character stories receive three levels each.
- Recurring guides, rivals, and bosses can share those chapters. Every listed character does not automatically receive three dedicated levels.
- The finale occurs at the sleeping sun.
- The sun is a gentle, warm, expressive living presence.
- Restoring the melody wakes it and brings a full dawn.
- Poppy and Puddlewick choose to return to separate homes because both homes still matter.
- Their farewell allows future visits; permanent or forced separation is not the selected ending.

## 4. Overarching Story Spine

This outline connects the confirmed decisions without finalizing individual scenes.

### Beginning: An Unexpected Meeting

Separate encounters with Zuzu draw Poppy and Puddlewick into the woven dimension. They meet and become involved in Auntie Aster's trouble. Helping her reveals the first fragment through Bloom.

The opening establishes their chemistry and shows that movement, living environments, and the morning song are connected.

**Open:** Whether the player sees both home universes before arrival, their exact first meeting, and Auntie Aster's initial problem.

### Middle: Following the Melody

Curiosity leads the pair through character-centred adventures. Each local conflict belongs to its inhabitants and environment. Resolving it and restoring Bloom reveals another part of the melody.

The journey builds recurring relationships while the protagonists discover how the fragments belong together. Poppy remains her curious, kind self. Puddlewick's relationships gradually show him that he is valued without needing a grand reputation.

**Open:** Chapter roster, order, individual conflicts, fragment distribution, and the moment when the pair understands the whole quest.

### Finale: The Morning Returns

Friends reunite as the journey reaches the sleeping sun. The recovered melody becomes complete, wakes the sun, and restores dawn across the dimension.

The finale is a moment of wonder and shared accomplishment. Its exact movement sequence, musical arrangement, staging, and dialogue remain open.

### Farewell: Two Homes, One Friendship

Poppy and Puddlewick return to their separate lives because those lives still matter to them. The parting carries gratitude and sadness, and leaves future visits possible.

**Proposed, not confirmed:** Zuzu provides the routes home, giving the ending a connection to the opening.

## 5. Chapter and Movement Direction

### Confirmed Narrative Structure

- Three levels per selected main story.
- Each story connects a character's trouble to an environment and a recovered melody fragment.
- Other characters can recur within the same story.
- Short scenes and environmental expression support the movement-led experience.

### Preserved Source Design Goals

These come from the supplied material and need chapter-specific design work:
- Build levels around Cloud Curl, pumping, rolling bowls, slope launches, cloud skimming, aerial shortcuts, rhythm, and timed movement.
- Increase difficulty progressively and consider assists.
- Keep boss encounters spectacular and grounded in movement mastery.
- Clearly signal dangerous states, temporary erased routes, timed gates, rotating lanes, and other hazards.
- Treat troublesome creatures as inhabitants with motives and habits.
- Preserve the prototype's cloud-skating beauty as an environment concept.
- Preserve speedrunning, optional routes, and movement-mastery challenges alongside the story.

**Proposed chapter template:** introduce the character and trouble; deepen the trouble through exploration; resolve it through a movement encounter and restoration. This three-part beat structure has not been separately approved.

## 6. Character Roster from the Main Source File

Source: "02 - Auntie Aster.md" (the supplied filename uses an em dash).
The original file's numbering is inconsistent, so characters are organized by name here.

Except where confirmed decisions above specify otherwise, appearances, personalities, gameplay roles, and story hooks below are **source concepts**, not final art specifications or a finalized chapter roster.

### Auntie Aster - The Flower Who Raised the Garden

- Appearance: a plump grandmotherly flower with a huge sunflower bonnet, porcelain watering can, leafy apron, mismatched gardening boots, and petals that change colour with her mood.
- Personality: overprotective, nosy, endlessly affectionate; believes tea, sunshine, and a proper breakfast can solve almost anything.
- Role concept: welcomes players at gardens and checkpoints, demonstrates basic techniques comically, and offers hints after repeated mistakes.
- Confirmed story role: the first friend whose problem draws the protagonists into the adventure.
- Open: her specific problem and three-level story.

### Lady Lilt - The Dandelion Dancer

- Appearance: a tall, delicate dandelion ballerina with a floating circular seed-petal tutu, tiny leaf slippers, and ribbon-like arms.
- Personality: graceful, encouraging, unexpectedly competitive; treats even falling over as part of a dance.
- Role concept: teaches rhythm and Cloud Curl pumping through choreography; petals trace a valley's momentum line.
- Transcript idea: a dedicated character story was suggested. Its inclusion and setting remain open.

### Rollo Rumble - The Fastest Beetle Alive (According to Rollo)

- Appearance: a squat bronze beetle with a polished round shell, tiny racing goggles, oversized sneakers, and a flaming flower-patterned scarf.
- Personality: loud, overconfident, competitive, and genuinely delighted when beaten.
- Role concept: recurring speedrun rival with ghosts through bowls, slope launches, and aerial shortcuts; becomes increasingly impressed by the player.
- Transcript direction: retain him as a recurring character. Stained-glass styling was floated, followed by an opalescent environment idea; neither is finalized.

### Tick & Tock - The Clockwork Cuckoo Brothers

- Appearance: two mechanical birds with clockwork key backs, pendulum tails, and mismatched hats; one coral and gold, the other mint and lavender.
- Personality: Tick is obsessed with punctuality; Tock is chronically late. They argue in synchronized chirps.
- Role concept: comedic hosts of timed gates, rotating platforms, and precision traversal trials.
- Open: their home environment and whether they host recurring challenges or a main story.

### Mobius Mabel - The Cat Who Has No Other Side

- Appearance: a lavender-and-coral striped cat whose impossibly long body forms a continuous Mobius loop; her head may emerge from her tail, or she may walk through herself.
- Personality: sleepy, wise, casually absurd; riddles make sense much later.
- Role concept: guides looping shortcuts and secret Cloud Curl routes; her silhouette traces momentum paths.
- Transcript note: an additional visual remark is unclear and must not be treated as an approved redesign.

### Zuzu the Zipperfish - Keeper of the Little Universe

- Appearance: an enormous turquoise fish swimming through the air, with a golden belly zipper opening onto miniature moons, stars, and drifting galaxies.
- Personality: exceptionally polite, philosophical, and afraid of getting lost inside himself.
- Role concept: opens temporary windows onto secret chambers, collectibles, and surreal bonus environments.
- Confirmed story role: separate encounters with his inner universe bring Poppy and Puddlewick into the same dimension.
- Proposed: a return-home role. Open: his intentions, arrival mechanics, and recurring appearances.

### Oona the Upside-Down Orchard - The Tree That Dreams of Walking

- Appearance: a wandering apple tree with upward-growing roots like antlers, tiny smiling planetary apples, sleepy eyes in her bark, and floating patterned leaves.
- Personality: ancient, gentle, dreamy; a hundred years is a reasonable afternoon nap.
- Role concept: planetary fruit activates magical garden sequences, and her orchard transforms into dancing constellations.
- Open: her story and the relationship between orchard and cosmic environments.

### Socktopus - The Eight-Legged Dance Disaster

- Appearance: a bright peach octopus on land, wearing a different sock and roller skate on every tentacle; limbs tangle into impossible knots.
- Personality: flamboyant; considers himself the greatest dancer despite spectacular evidence otherwise.
- Role concept: recurring comic host of sliding slopes, timed jumps, dance challenges, and chain reactions.

### Lady Loophole - The Door That Goes Everywhere Except Home

- Original appearance: a turquoise doorway on pink high heels, an eye in her brass keyhole, and a striped curtain resembling a tongue.
- Personality: mischievous, mysterious, and prone to questionable travel advice.
- Role concept: optional pocket-dimension challenges with surreal presentation and authored traversal puzzles.
- Source revision request: the transcript explicitly calls for changing her description because it feels creepy. Preserve the original as reference, not as approved final appearance.
- Her role as the cause of the protagonists' arrival was not selected.

### Bongo Bottomless - The Drum Who Forgot His Bottom

- Appearance: a walking drum with legs disappearing into endless spirals, a face across the drumhead, and visible coloured sound rings with each step.
- Personality: excessively friendly, impulsive, and incapable of staying quiet.
- Role concept: rhythm and movement-mastery tutor; sound waves mark springcap beats, launch timings, and musical platform sequences.
- Transcript emphasis: a helper/tutorial character.

### Prince Prickle - The Royal Thorn of Absolutely Nowhere

- Appearance: a miniature hedgehog-like ink-thistle creature with a crooked golden crown, burgundy leaf cape, and enormous frown.
- Personality: easily offended, self-important, deeply lonely; declares almost everything his kingdom.
- Role concept: recurring mini-boss rolling down slopes in a visibly dangerous spiky ball, creating jump-timing and evasive-momentum encounters.
- Story hook: reveals a gentle side.
- Transcript direction: proposed as the first enemy. The exact opening encounter and ordering remain to be settled.

### The Eraser Eels - Hungry Little Mistakes

- Original appearance: long rubbery cream-coloured eels with pencil-shaving fins, huge teeth, and ink-stained noses.
- Personality: silly, insatiably hungry, unaware that eating scenery inconveniences others.
- Role concept: briefly erase magical pathways before they reappear; actions need clear warnings.
- Source revision request: retain the concept while developing a different appearance.

### Countess Stillwater - The Keeper of Perfect Reflections

- Appearance: an elegant translucent jellyfish-like woman with a porcelain face, sapphire gown, glass parasol, and luminous tendrils resembling musical notation.
- Personality: poetic, lonely, hypnotically calm; wants to preserve beautiful moments forever.
- Story hook: believes movement destroys beauty because ripples spoil perfect reflections.
- Encounter concept: water-skimming, expanding ripples, reflected pathways, and currents.
- Source environment direction: cnidarian biome. Moonsea was an earlier label; the discussion moved away from assigning her to fungi.

### The Bureau of Beige - Department of Unnecessary Normality

- Appearance: tiny grey bureaucrats with rectangular heads, identical ties, huge rubber stamps, and oversized glasses.
- Personality: joyless, officious, frightened by irregular shapes; favour "standardized."
- Role concept: remove colour by stamping areas into temporary monochrome patterns, then allow momentum and Bloom to reawaken them.
- Transcript emphasis: enthusiasm for black-and-white transformations.
- They are not established as a central organization controlling every antagonist.

### The Crooked Carousel - The Ride That Refuses to End

- Appearance: a colossal living carousel with mismatched animal heads, spinning stained-glass eyes, accordion legs, and an organ-pipe torso. Horses can become fish, stars, or melting chess pieces.
- Personality: manically joyful, desperate for applause, secretly afraid of abandonment.
- Encounter concept: carnival chase across rotating, clearly marked lanes, with launch rings, clouds, springcaps, and increasingly wild music.
- Transcript emphasis: spectacular and mind-bending action with a playful feel.
- Open: its local conflict and resolution.

### The Taxonomist - The Man Who Tried to Alphabetize the Sky

- Appearance: a gigantic walking filing cabinet with blinking drawer-eyes, paperclip legs, a chequered tie, and a rotating square jaw.
- Personality: obsessive, pompous, frustrated by spontaneity, offended by curves.
- Role concept: imposes rectangles, grids, and repetitions; authored slopes and circular routes let players counter his organization.
- Source environment direction: fungi biome, turning mushrooms into squares and rectangles.
- Transcript history: a sequel was briefly suggested before the fungi placement; release scope has not been finalized.

### The Honeycomb Hypnotist - The Bee That Dreams in Fractals

- Appearance: a massive honeybee with iridescent rainbow wings, glass eyes containing rotating mandalas, and a honeycomb crown dripping luminous honey.
- Personality: serene, persuasive, convinced everyone should think the same thoughts.
- Encounter concept: kaleidoscopic aerial movement through expanding hexagons, moving hazards, and unfolding routes, while core movement controls stay consistent.
- Open: whether it receives a main chapter, supporting encounter, or later expansion.

### The Negative King - Ruler of Everything That Isn't There

- Appearance: a silhouette cut out of the storybook sky; a different universe is visible through his outline, with stars, strange moons, and upside-down landscapes.
- Personality: gentle, mysterious, melancholic, quietly jealous of others' colours.
- Encounter concept: a pursuit through negative-space scenery with clear physical routes and shifting distant skies.
- Story hook: help him find his own colours.
- Transcript emphasis: preserve the universe-through-his-outline concept and avoid presenting him as strongly evil.
- He is not established as the overarching villain.

- **Superseded:** silence caused the worlds to tangle. The user revisited this question and selected natural weaving.
- The weaving itself breaking the song was not selected.
- A living chorus or daily communal ritual as the song's nature was not selected; the selected form is a scattered melody.
- A single magical accident as the cause of fragmentation was not selected; independent local troubles are the chosen cause.
- A central mastermind tying every antagonist together was not selected.
- Finding a way home as the primary quest was not selected.
- Poppy being restless, lonely, or requiring a major corrective arc was not selected.
- Puddlewick as a duck-oil salesman is superseded by his self-appointed knight direction.
- Duck-oil production jokes were rejected in the source conversation and are not story material.
- Lady Loophole or natural crossings causing the initial arrivals were not selected; Zuzu's little universe is the selected mechanism.
- Every character receiving a dedicated three-level story was not selected.
- A permanently travelling group was not selected; friends mainly remain in their regions and reunite.
- A shared permanent home, continued travel as the ending, and uncertain forced separation were not selected.
- Planetary rings or Aster's garden as the finale destination were not selected.
- A purely impersonal sun or a separately troubled sun character was not selected.
